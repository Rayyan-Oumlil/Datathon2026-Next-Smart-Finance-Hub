# ETF (exchange-traded fund)

An ETF is a fund whose units trade on a stock exchange during the day, like a share. One unit holds a small part of every asset in the fund.

- A broad-index ETF holds the companies of a wide index, for example the S&P 500 (about 500 large US companies) or the S&P/TSX 60 (60 large Canadian companies).
- A sector or theme ETF holds companies from one area only, so its price tends to move more than a broad-index fund.
- An ETF charges a yearly fee called the management expense ratio (MER), taken from the fund.
- The price of an ETF follows the value of what it holds; it can go down as well as up.

In this app, "blue-chip ETF" means a fund on our short list of broad-index funds. The list and each fund's issuer page are in the repository.
